Incident Severity Levels and Service Level Agreements

This policy defines how the IT Service Desk prioritises incidents and the response and resolution targets (SLAs) that apply to each priority. It applies to all incidents raised through the Service Desk portal, by phone on extension 4357, or by email to servicedesk@halcyon-dynamics.example.

Priority definitions

Priority is set from impact (how many people or how critical the service) and urgency (how quickly the business is harmed).

  • P1 – Critical: a business-critical service is unavailable for many users or a whole site, or there is an active security breach. Examples: company-wide Email outage, VPN down for all remote staff, ransomware detected.
  • P2 – High: a critical service is degraded, or a single critical user or team cannot work, with no workaround. Examples: Finance cannot run payroll, a compromised account.
  • P3 – Medium: a single user or small group is affected and a workaround exists, or a non-critical service is down. Examples: one user's Outlook will not open, a printer fault.
  • P4 – Low: minor issues, questions, and standard service requests. Examples: how-to questions, a software install request.

Raising a P1 or P2

P1 and P2 incidents must be reported by phone on extension 4357 so that the on-call engineer is paged immediately. Do not rely on a portal ticket or email alone for a P1. During a P1, the current status is published on https://status.halcyon-dynamics.example, and a Major Incident Manager coordinates the response.

Escalation

If an SLA target is at risk, the ticket is escalated automatically to the team lead at 75% of the target time and to the IT Service Manager at 100%. Requesters may also ask for an escalation by replying to the ticket with the word ESCALATE in the subject line.

Reopening tickets

A resolved ticket closes automatically after 5 business days. Until then, it can be reopened by replying to the resolution email. After closure, a new ticket must be raised that references the old ticket number.
